sidera constellation catalogue

Adapted from d3-celestial, Copyright (c) 2015 Olaf Frohn, BSD-3-Clause.
Upstream: https://github.com/ofrohn/d3-celestial
Files: data/constellations.lines.json, data/constellations.json,
       data/stars.8.json; downloaded 2026-11-08.
Full upstream license: d3-celestial-LICENSE.txt.

The upstream star coordinates and magnitudes derive from XHIP:
Anderson E., Francis C. (2012), VizieR V/137D.
https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/V/137D
The positions are equatorial J2000.0. SIDERA retains 759 stars: the 757
used by the 743 figure segments, plus Epsilon Crucis (HIP 60260) and
Sigma Octantis (HIP 104382). These two isolated points were added from the
same downloaded stars.8.json snapshot on 2026-11-10; no new segments.
This is not the entire magnitude-limited catalogue.

Names and three-letter abbreviations follow the IAU list:
https://www.iau.org/IAU/Astronomy-FAQs/Constellations.aspx
Changes: merge both Serpens figures under Serpens [Ser], use the IAU name
Corona Australis [CrA], preserve Bootes' diaeresis in the custom font.
No IAU boundaries are drawn. Line figures are the d3-celestial variant;
IAU does not prescribe an official set of connecting lines.

tools/constellations.py converts the normalized JSON into Q14 unit vectors,
star size classes, segment indices and single-row labels. Catalogue vectors
are streamed in small batches from sidera.dat; projected coordinates and
segment indices are held in XRAM. Labels and star sizes are also held in XRAM;
rendered pixels and the font remain exclusively in XRAM.

The stellar model uses the linear terms of Lieske's IAU 1976 precession
angles, with the local sidereal angle used by sidera's solar/lunar model.
It omits proper motion, annual parallax, aberration, nutation and atmospheric
refraction. The fixed-point implementation is original sidera code; it is
not IAU SOFA software and carries no IAU/SOFA endorsement.

The offline test compares against full cubic precession and a standard GMST
polynomial in double precision. It measures numerical/model approximation,
not the omitted physical effects or catalogue astrometric errors.
