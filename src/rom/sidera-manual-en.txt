SIDERA - The Celestial Observatory
Standalone user manual

1. ABOUT SIDERA

SIDERA is a sky observatory for Picocomputer 6502 with VGA. It shows the Sun,
Moon, six planets and stars from all 88 constellations for an observer on
Earth. Choose a place and a date between 1900 and 2200, explore the sky,
follow daily paths, and check local sunrise, sunset, moonrise and moonset.
Calculations run on the 6502 without an Internet connection. The standalone
edition runs without razemOS(mt) or mtRAM. Its display is 640 x 480 pixels.

2. STARTING SIDERA

Connect a VGA display and keyboard. Copy sidera.rp6502 to your USB drive.
At the Picocomputer monitor, enter:

  LOAD MSC0:/sidera.rp6502

The ROM includes the graphics, city catalogue, time zones, help and manual.
No separate data files are needed. An overlay load error ends the program
with a diagnostic message and returns to the monitor. After a five-second
splash, the sky opens.
Wait for Working... to disappear before judging a fully calculated view.
Use Q to return to the monitor. The computer clock supplies LIVE time.

Without saved settings, the observer is Warsaw [PL], 113 m AMSL. FOV is 120
degrees, the view centre is 24 degrees above the horizon, and G/T/C/P are off.
The initial direction follows the current local hour: E at 04:00-09:59,
S at 10:00-15:59, W at 16:00-21:59, and N at 22:00-03:59.

Optional startup parameters:
  /location NAME       Country code, city or CC/City; quote names with spaces.
  /date "YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS"   UTC date and time; starts in SIM / PAUSE.
  /live                Use the current UTC time.
  /view AZ,ALT          Azimuth 0-359, altitude -54 to +54; whole degrees.
  /fov N               Horizontal field of view: 50-160, in steps of 10.
  /grid N              Grid mode 0-3; 0 means off.
  /paths on|off        Show or hide the daily Sun and Moon paths.
  /planets on|off      Show or hide the planets.
  /constellations N    Constellation mode 0-4; 0 means off.
  /config PATH         Read and save settings at this path.
  /assets PATH         Use an external sidera.dat instead of the embedded one.
  /help or /?          Print brief startup help and return to the monitor.

Examples (each command is entered on one line):
  LOAD MSC0:/sidera.rp6502 /location "PL/Gdansk"
  LOAD MSC0:/sidera.rp6502 /date "1945-09-01 02:45:00"
  LOAD MSC0:/sidera.rp6502 /view 65,14 /fov 160
  LOAD MSC0:/sidera.rp6502 /config MSC0:/evening.cfg

Settings are applied in this order: defaults, configuration, startup options.
The last repeated option wins, including the choice between /date and /live.
An explicit /location chooses the local-hour direction unless /view is given.
Otherwise a saved View is retained. Use a configuration for a complete preset.
Country and city names ignore case; use ASCII spellings such as Gdansk.

3. KEYBOARD CONTROLS

Left / Right        Turn the view by 5 degrees.
Up / Down           Raise/lower the view centre by 5 degrees, within +/-54.
LCtrl+Left/Right     Narrow/widen FOV by 10 degrees, within 50-160.
Shift+Right         Next direction: N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW.
Shift+Left          Previous compass direction; 50 -> 45 -> 0 -> 315 degrees.
N                   Return to LIVE using the computer's current UTC time.
Space               Cycle LIVE -> SIM/PAUSE -> SIM/PLAY -> LIVE.
+ / -               Move forward/backward 15 minutes and pause simulation.
[ / ]               Move backward/forward one day and pause simulation.
D                   Edit UTC date and time.
L                   Edit latitude, longitude and height in metres AMSL.
K                   Open Cities to select an observer location.
Z                   Edit the IANA time zone, for example Europe/Warsaw.
S                   Save all current settings to the configuration file.
G                   Cycle grid modes: scale, crosses, dots, off.
T                   Toggle Sun and Moon daily paths.
C                   Cycle constellation modes described below.
P                   Toggle Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn and Uranus.
Ctrl+L              Redraw the screen.
X                   Show/hide a Solar System snapshot for the displayed UTC.
Q / Esc             Quit to the monitor when no editor or city list is open.

Space freezes the displayed UTC time when leaving LIVE. SIM/PLAY advances
in 5-minute steps; returning to LIVE immediately reads the computer clock.
N still returns directly to LIVE. FOV uses the physical left Ctrl key only;
right Ctrl (also when held together with left Ctrl) does not change FOV.
H and Shift+H have no action.

In an editor, Enter applies changes and Esc cancels. Left/Right, Home/End,
Backspace and Delete edit the text. In L, type D for degrees, an apostrophe
for minutes and a double quote for seconds. Use tenths of an arcsecond, e.g.
49d38'54.6" N, 19d09'19.7" E, 395 m AMSL. AMSL means Above Mean Sea Level;
heights range from -500 to 10000 m. IANA zone names are case-sensitive.
Cities occupies a 590 x 322 pixel panel inside the sky plot, with a 5-pixel
outer margin, a 1-pixel white frame and a black-on-white CITIES label.
The surrounding display stays visible. Search, 14 rows per page, coordinates,
result count, messages and keyboard help all fit inside the frame.
In Cities, type a country code, country or city name; filtering starts at two
characters. Use Up/Down, Page Up/Down or Home/End; Enter selects, Esc cancels.
A selected city supplies its coordinates, height and time zone. Selection
also chooses the local-hour viewing direction; press S to keep the settings.

The X panel shows a schematic Solar System from ecliptic north. Eight
concentric circles separate the planet sprites; distances are not in AU
scale. The Sun uses its original 32 x 32 sprite. Planet directions follow
the date printed at the top right. The panel is 317 x 317 pixels.
English planet names appear beside their sprites, without connecting lines;
their placement avoids other names, sprites and the panel edges.
An unlabelled white 2 x 2 dot marks the Moon, 8 pixels from Earth's centre.
A one-pixel black border separates it from the orbit lines. Its direction
follows the snapshot time; labels avoid both the dot and its border.
Only X and Q work while it is open. Sky drawing and calculations stop;
LIVE updates only the UTC/LOCAL clocks. SIM / PLAY pauses until X closes
the panel, then resumes. Closing recalculates and redraws the sky for the
current time. Reopening takes a new snapshot. Q returns to the monitor.

4. USING THE OBSERVATORY

Time is UTC; the line below it is LOCAL time for the selected time zone,
including daylight saving where applicable. Location gives coordinates and
height. View gives azimuth and elevation of the centre; FOV is the horizontal
angular span. Azimuth runs clockwise: N=0, E=90, S=180, W=270 degrees.
Altitude is positive above the dotted horizon and negative below it.
Sun/Moon AZ and ALT give their positions. Moon LIT is the illuminated percent;
its image follows the phase and tilt relative to the local horizon.
Object icons are illustrative and do not represent their angular diameters.

Rise and Set are local times for the displayed LOCAL calendar date.
..:.. means calculation is in progress; --:-- means no such event that day.
UP means above the horizon all day; DOWN means below it all day.
N/A means the event times are unavailable. Times assume a level horizon
and standard refraction; buildings, hills and weather can change visibility.

G cycles: 1 = degree labels and edge ticks; 2 = these plus crosses at grid
intersections; 3 = these plus dots every 2 degrees; 0 = off.
C cycles: 1 = stars; 2 = stars and dotted figures; 3 = figures with Latin
names and IAU abbreviations in white on black; 4 = only Ursa Major, Ursa
Minor,
Crux, Centaurus and Octans, with stars and labels but no connecting lines;
0 = off. Epsilon Crucis and Sigma Octantis are included as isolated stars.
Mode 4 also marks Merak, Dubhe, Polaris, Alpha Centauri, Beta Centauri,
Gacrux, Acrux and Sigma Octantis with open 9x9 corner frames and white names
on black. Star names take priority over constellation labels; labels that
cannot fit without overlapping are omitted. Alpha Centauri uses the
catalogue position of component B (HIP 71681).
Figures can extend beyond the view and are also drawn below the horizon.
The footer groups time and settings in its first row, and view controls and
panels in its second row. Active G/T/C/P labels are highlighted in inverse video.
T shows paths from midnight to midnight UTC: solid for Sun, dashed for Moon.

LIVE updates the clock each second, Sun/Moon/planets each minute, and stars
every five minutes. SIM / PLAY advances in 5-minute steps when calculations
and drawing finish, with at least 0.336 seconds between steps. Detailed views
can take substantially longer. No intermediate instants are skipped and no
positions are interpolated. Manual +/- still uses 15-minute steps and pauses
playback. SIM / PAUSE holds the date. Working... marks calculation or
drawing in progress. Paths and rise/set calculations can take a while.

S saves the location, zone, View, FOV, G/T/C/P and time mode. The default file
is MSC0:/sidera.cfg; /config selects another file. A writable drive is
needed.
LIVE reloads with current time; a saved simulation reloads at its saved UTC
date in PAUSE, even if saved during PLAY. Successful saving clears the bottom
message. Changes are not saved automatically, and older formats are not read.

Use SIDERA for sky exploration, not precision eclipse or navigation work.
Monitor command reference: https://picocomputer.github.io/pico.html

Have fun!

Astronomical models and reference material

Sun, Moon and planetary calculations use a fixed-point adaptation of Paul
Schlyter's published orbital elements, Kepler solution and perturbations:
  Paul Schlyter, "How to compute planetary positions"
  https://stjarnhimlen.se/comp/ppcomp.html
  https://stjarnhimlen.se/comp/tutorial.html
Schlyter credits T. van Flandern and K. Pulkkinen's "Low precision formulae
for planetary positions" (1980) as the basis of his simplified algorithms.
SIDERA's numerical implementation and approximations are its own; this is
not a redistribution of a third-party astronomical calculation library.

Stellar precession uses the linear terms of the IAU 1976 precession model
associated with J. H. Lieske and collaborators. Constellation names and
three-letter abbreviations follow the International Astronomical Union:
  https://www.iau.org/IAU/Astronomy-FAQs/Constellations.aspx
The line figures are not official IAU boundaries or prescribed IAU figures.
No IAU SOFA software is included, and no IAU/SOFA endorsement is claimed.

Rise/set conventions follow the U.S. Naval Observatory's definitions:
  https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/RST_defs
Independent development tests use USNO event tables and NASA/JPL Horizons
DE441 reference positions. These are test references, not a runtime
ephemeris database or a dependency on an online service:
  https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/RS_OneDay
  https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/
  https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html

  https://github.com/ofrohn/d3-celestial
Source files: constellations.lines.json, constellations.json, stars.8.json.
Snapshot downloaded on 2026-11-08. SIDERA selects 759 stars and 743 segments,
including Epsilon Crucis (HIP 60260) and Sigma Octantis (HIP 104382) as

converts coordinates to Q14 J2000 unit vectors, assigns star size classes,

Python tzdata 2026.6 package. SIDERA expands UTC-offset transitions over
1900-2200 and converts them to its own binary interval tables.
